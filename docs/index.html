1 · The product

Premium Mini is Spotify's short-term pass for India. At launch it cost ₹7 for a day or ₹25 for a week.1 It gives ad-free listening, but it works on mobile only, and offline downloads are capped at 30 songs on one device.2 The full Premium Individual plan cost ₹119 a month at the time of this project.3

The insight that shapes the plan: the weekly pass is cheaper per day than the monthly plan (₹3.57 vs ₹3.97). Four weeks of passes cost ₹100, or about ₹107 for 30 days. So the upgrade from Mini to monthly cannot be sold on price. It has to be sold on what Mini lacks: desktop and web access, unlimited downloads, and not having to renew every week.

2 · Who we target

Students

18–24, on a tight budget, already stream daily. Want ad-free listening during exams and commutes.

Occasion listeners

Want Premium for a road trip, a wedding, or a house party, not every month.

Casual free users

Tolerate ads today. A ₹7 day pass shows them what ad-free feels like.

Mobile-first, Tier 2–3 cities

Pay with UPI, prefer small prepaid amounts, and have no reason to want desktop access.

All four groups share three traits: they are price-sensitive, they dislike ads, and they live on their phones. Mini's mobile-only limit doesn't hurt them.

  • Instagram and YouTube get half the budget because short video is where students and occasion listeners spend their time. Retargeting reaches people who visited the plan page but didn't buy.
  • Creators get 20% because peer recommendation drives app choice for this audience. Creators show the product in a real moment, like a road-trip playlist.
  • Search gets 15% to capture people already looking for "cheap Spotify Premium" or "Spotify ad-free".
  • CRM and in-app get only 5% because they cost almost nothing per message. Existing free users are the cheapest audience to convert, and push, email and in-app banners reach them without buying media.

Moments to plan around: Holi, the IPL season, wedding season, and summer travel. Each is a short window when an occasion listener wants Premium for a few days, which is exactly what Mini sells. The 5% reserve funds bursts around these moments.

6 · Campaign assets

Social ad copy

"Road trip this weekend? ₹25 covers the whole week. No ads, no interruptions."

"Exam week. Your playlist, without the ads. Try Premium Mini from ₹7."

Display & search copy

"Ad-free Spotify from ₹7 a day. Pay with UPI. No subscription."

"Going offline? Save up to 30 songs with Premium Mini."

Visuals

Phone screen showing an ad-free queue; a 6-second Reel cutting from an ad break to uninterrupted music; carousel of "moments" (trip, wedding, exam).

Upgrade messages (to Mini buyers)

"Your pass ends tonight. Keep listening on every device with Premium."

"Bought 3 weeks in a row? Go monthly and stop renewing."

Every claim in the copy matches what Mini actually includes. The copy never promises desktop access or unlimited downloads.

Reading the base case: the campaign brings in about 16,320 new paying users at roughly ₹260 each. Each payer is worth about ₹146 in gross revenue, so paid media alone does not pay back within six months. To break even, the plan-page purchase rate would need to reach about 14% instead of the assumed 8%. Only the bull case clears that bar.

Why this replaces the "3X users" claim: the original draft said users would grow up to 3X. That can't be checked without Spotify's current Mini subscriber count, so this version estimates the number of new paying users and shows the math behind it.

8 · Recommendations

  1. Lead with owned channels. In-app banners and push messages to existing free users cost almost nothing, so they lower the blended CPA the most. Scale them first.
  2. Treat launch as a test. Use weeks 5–6 to check each channel's CPA against the ₹146 break-even, then move the remaining 70% of paid media to the channels that beat it.
  3. Grow repeat purchases. Each extra weekly pass per payer adds ₹25 of revenue. A loyalty nudge (for example, a discount after several passes) is cheaper than buying new users.
  4. Sell the upgrade on features, not price. Weekly passes are cheaper per day than monthly, so the upgrade message should stress all-device access, unlimited downloads and not having to renew.

9 · Risks and limits

  • Cannibalization: some monthly subscribers could switch to weekly passes and pay Spotify less. Track downgrades alongside new payers.
  • Mobile only: Mini cannot reach desktop listeners, so desktop placements are wasted spend.
  • Assumed numbers: the budget, CPM and funnel rates are illustrative. Real campaign data would replace them after the test phase.
  • Prices change: prices shown are launch-era figures. Spotify has since changed its India pricing, so they should be checked before any real use.
